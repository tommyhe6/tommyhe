So what exactly is \( \Omicron \)?

I've always disliked how handwavy \( \Omicron \) tends to be, especially amongst programmersThis is a sidenote.. Some common handwavy statements you might hear include:

  • Sorting is \( \Omicron(n \log n) \).
  • \( \Omicron(1) \) means constant time algorithms.
  • \( \Omicron \) represents how long an algorithm takes to run.
  • \( \Omicron \) represents the rate of growth between the time of an algorithm and the input size.
  • \( \Omicron \) represents an upper bound on how fast an algorithm can be.

None of these are wrong per se, but many questions arise kj:

  • What does the \( n \) mean above?
  • What is the base for the \( \log \) and does that matter?
  • What is the connection between the expressions \( n \log n \) and algorithms?
  • What is the relation between \( \Omicron \) and worst case analysis?
  • What is the assumed computation model and does that matter?

The goal here is to end with a clear and precise understanding of \( \Omicron \) and what people precisely mean when we bring it up. Before, we bridge our understanding though, we have to lay some foundations.

The Theory

First, let \( F = \left\{f : \mathbb{R} \rightarrow \mathbb{R} \right\} \). \( F \) is the set of all the functions we are interested in for now.

(\( \Omicron \) of real-valued functions with 1 argument).

\( \Omicron: F \rightarrow 2^F \) is the set-valued function defined by \[ \Omicron(f) = \left\{g \in F \mid \exists C \in \mathbb{R}_{> 0}, N \in \mathbb{R}, \left\lvert g(x) \right\rvert \le C \left\lvert f(x) \right\rvert \forall x \ge N \right\}. \]

Some things to note immediately:

  1. Only the the eventual behavior of the function matters for \( \Omicron \) - the behavior at some finite number of specific points does not.
  2. There is no mention of "cases" here, meaning \( \Omicron \) is orthogonal to worse case or average case analysis. The correct viewpoint here is to view \( \Omicron \) as sets or classes of functions.

Let \( f(x) = x \) and \( g(x) = x^2 \). Then, \( f \in \Omicron(g) . \)

Notice that \( \forall x \ge 1 \), both \( x^2 \) and \( x \) are positive, and so \( \left\lvert x \right\rvert \le 1 \cdot \left\lvert x^2 \right\rvert \).

(Eventually \( 0 \) functions)

\( \forall f \in F \) that is eventually \( 0 \), we have \( f \in \Omicron(g) \forall g \in F \).
\( \forall f \in F \) that is eventually \( 0 \), we have \( \Omicron(f) = \{ g \in \Omicron \mid g \text{ is eventually } 0\} = \Omicron(0) \).

Let \( f(x) = 1 \) and \( g(x) = \left\lvert \sin(x) \right\rvert + 1 \). Then, \( \Omicron(f) = \Omicron(g) \).

(Eventually bounded and bounded away from zero functions)

\( \forall f \in F \) that is eventually bounded and bounded away from zero, we have \( f \in \Omicron(1) \).

\[ \left\lvert f \right\rvert \le \left\lvert g \right\rvert \text{ eventually } \Rightarrow f \in \Omicron(g)) \]
